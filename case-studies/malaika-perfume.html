01 — Project overview

A branded sticker, presented like a fragrance.

Malaika Perfume needed retail packaging for a round branded sticker. On its own, a sticker is flat and easy to overlook. The brief was to give it the presence of a premium fragrance gift: framed, protected and clearly branded before the customer opens anything.

The solution is a four-part nested system. The sticker sits in a 3 × 3 in product box. A cross-fold chair insert holds that box, and positions it directly behind the square window of a 4 × 1.5 × 4 in display carton.

This case study follows the project from the original Illustrator dielines through structure, fit analysis, artwork, prepress and 3D presentation.

02 — Design objectives

What the packaging must achieve

01

Frame the brand

Show the full Malaika logo through the window, with no insert edges or gaps visible.

02

Hold the product centred

Keep the product box fixed in all three directions, with no sliding, tilting or rattle.

03

Protect a flat product

Keep the sticker flat and clean inside a rigid, closable box.

04

Stay efficient to produce

Use standard folding cartons and a glue-free insert that can be packed by hand.

05

Feel premium

A restrained navy, ivory and gold palette taken directly from the logo.

03 — Requirements & challenges

The constraints

VisibilityThe product box face must be slightly larger than the window, so only printed artwork shows.
PositioningA 3 in box inside a 4 in carton leaves about 0.5 in of free space on each side, so it needs a support.
DepthA 1.2 in box inside a 1.5 in carton must not move forward or back.
ProductA thin Ø 2.74 in sticker must stay flat and unmarked.
ProductionWork within the existing dielines without changing the structure.

05 — Brand artwork

One identity across every panel

Navy and ivory come directly from the Malaika logo. Champagne gold is the only accent, reserved for hairline frames and small type, so the logo always leads.

Logo preparation

The logo was supplied as a PNG. It was traced into vector outlines for print. Counters and crossbars, including the open “A” forms in MALAIKA, were checked letter by letter. “PERFUME” was re-set in a matching serif because the source pixels were too small to trace accurately.

10 — Folding & assembly

Six steps, packed by hand

01

Prepare the insert

Remove the flat insert from the sheet and pre-crease the walls and seat flaps.

02

Fold the chair

Fold the four walls back 90°, then fold the seat flaps back to open the 3.06 in seat.

03

Fill the product box

Erect the box, lock the base and lay the sticker flat inside.

04

Close the box

Tuck the lid. The logo face becomes the front of the unit.

05

Seat it in the insert

Press the box, lid forward, into the seat. The flaps hold its sides.

06

Load the carton

Slide the unit in behind the window panel and tuck the top lid closed.

13 — Window overlap: verified vs proposed

Closing the gap to the 2 mm intent

Verified original dieline
Window2.944 × 2.944 in · 74.78 mm
Overlap1.42 mm overall · 0.71 mm per side
NoteRead as a nominal 2.94 in, the overlap is 1.524 mm overall (0.762 mm per side)
Proposed revision — not in production file
Window2.9213 × 2.9213 in · 74.20 mm
Overlap2.00 mm overall · 1.00 mm per side
ConditionProduct box stays exactly 3.00 × 3.00 in

The supplied dieline gives about 1.4 mm of total overlap, below the intended 2 mm. Reducing the window to 2.9213 in restores 1 mm per side and gives more tolerance against print and die-cut variation. The change has not been applied to any production file. It must be checked against the editable Illustrator dieline and a physical sample before it is released.

14 — Mockup development

Built from the measured dimensions

Geometry

Every part is modelled at true size: 4 × 1.5 × 4, 3.93 × 1.45, 3 × 3 × 1.2 and Ø 2.74 in. Panels have 0.5 mm board thickness, the window has a cut edge and the product box has its thumb notch and tuck flap.

Artwork mapping

The same vector artwork used on the dielines is mapped onto each panel at print resolution. A separate foil layer makes the gold read as metal, not flat colour.

What the renders show

Proportions, window framing and branding. They are concept visualisations, not photographs of manufactured packaging. Board spring-back, glue and tolerances still need a real sample.

15 — Sampling & quality control

What the physical sample must prove

Dielines and renders confirm proportions, not performance. Before production, a printed, cut and creased sample should be assembled and checked against these points.

This is a checklist of considerations. It does not report test results.
